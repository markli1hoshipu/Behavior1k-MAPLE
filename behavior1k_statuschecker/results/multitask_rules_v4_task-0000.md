1. move to - No arms anywhere in frame and the camera is still far back: the whole room is in view, including the right-hand wall shelf with the small framed photos sitting on it, and the red radio is a tiny distant blob whose stripes cannot be made out. Use only for these widest shots — if those small framed photos on the shelf are not themselves clearly visible, it is not move to.

2. pick up from - The default label for this task; use it for any armless frame that is not one of the widest shots described in rule 1, and for the lifted-object frames. Two looks: (a) no arms or grippers anywhere in the image, the camera has moved in on the seating area so the blue sectional spans most of the width, and the radio stands upright and edge-on on the grey tabletop as a clearly resolvable red slab with white/dark vertical stripes down its narrow face, with the round glowing white table lamp beside it — ceiling, downlights and the pendant lamp may still be visible in the earlier of these frames, and the corner shelf may still show at the right edge; none of that rules it out. The bright round disc on the table is that lamp, not a control dial, and with no gripper touching anything these frames are never press. (b) arms have entered and the radio is a narrow upright red-and-white striped slab held high, lifted clear of the tabletop so it overlaps the wood floor or sofa behind it, with the left arm only a short stub in the bottom-left corner. The earliest armless approach frames genuinely cannot be distinguished from "move to" in a still frame; default to pick up from, which is far more common.

3. press - The radio lies flat with its wide rounded red top face and large round dark dial facing the camera (a small green light once on), and a gripper is down on that face. If that dial face is not visible, it is not press.

4. place on - The radio is back down low on the grey tabletop beside the glowing round lamp, edge-on and striped again, while the left arm reaches in horizontally from the left edge at mid-height, elbow and open gripper visible; last frames show it standing free with both grippers withdrawn.
