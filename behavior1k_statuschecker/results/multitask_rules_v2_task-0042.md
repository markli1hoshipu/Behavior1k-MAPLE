1. chop - Onion on the wooden board is already in several separated pieces with pale cut faces; knife lowered or set aside. A whole pink onion means not chop.
2. move to - Pulled-back or motion-blurred view: floor, cabinets, or the whole kitchen fill the frame, nothing gripped or worked on, sink and board distant.
3. pick up from - A gripper is closed on an object (pink onion, white bowl, dark board, knife) holding it clear of the surface. Grasp and release look identical in one frame; choose this when the object is held high.
4. place in - Only when a gripper or the tipped board is directly over the white bowl's opening. Never choose it just because the bowl, sink, or board is visible; it is rare.
5. place on - A gripper holds the pink onion low, just above the otherwise empty wooden board.
6. place on next to - The white bowl or the board is being lowered, nearly touching the bare counter left of the sink, ending up beside the other.
7. pour - Board lifted and tilted toward the white bowl with pale cubes on it; no knife held.
8. push to - Red-handled knife held upright with its tip in or beside a still-whole pink onion, or hovering over bare counter right of the board. Not separable from chop in one frame; prefer push to while the onion is unbroken.
